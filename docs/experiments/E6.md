# E6 — Real-World Validation, Batch 1 (houseplants)

- **Date:** 8 November 2026
- **Method:** three user-supplied photos uploaded to the live app
  (https://leaf-disease-prediction-app.streamlit.app/) and diagnosed
  one at a time. No correctness judgement is possible: all three
  species are outside the model's 38 crop classes, so there is no
  ground-truth label. This batch measures behaviour, not accuracy.
- **Artefacts:** none (app output only; photos are the user's).

## Findings

1. **The gate is too strict on real-world leaf photos.** It rejected
   2 of 3 genuine leaves (7.5% and 38.1%). Its leaf recall of 99.74%
   (E4) was measured on clean PlantVillage lab photos, and its
   99.87% rejection on Caltech objects — neither measures real-world
   leaves against busy backgrounds (pot, window, wall). Real-world
   leaf recall is lower; n=3 is too small to measure it, but the
   direction is clear. A future improvement is retraining the gate
   with real-world leaves (varied backgrounds, lighting, angles).
2. **Unseen leaf species get confident wrong answers.** The
   philodendron — a species the model never saw — was labelled
   "Potato — Healthy" at 85.1% calibrated confidence with no
   uncertainty warning. The gate (99.8% leaf) and the energy OOD
   check both passed it: they catch *non-leaf* images, not *unseen
   leaf species*. A green leaf photo produces leaf-like logits, so
   the energy signal cannot tell a known species from an unknown
   one. This is the exact failure the audit warned about ("never
   present an unreliable prediction as a confirmed diagnosis") and
   it belongs in the paper as a stated limitation.
3. **The clean-background photo passed easily** (99.8%): the gate
   works well when the photo looks like the lab photos it trained
   on. The gap is backgrounds and species, not leaves as such.

## Limitations

- n=3, all houseplants, no ground truth — no accuracy number can be
  computed from this batch.
- No crop photos (tomato/apple/potato/pepper/corn) yet — those are
  needed for a scored real-world accuracy (the number the paper
  needs next to the 94.21% validation figure).

## Next

- More photos, especially crop leaves with known healthy/diseased
  labels, for a scored RW-Field accuracy.
- Consider a gate retraining round with real-world leaves (varied
  backgrounds) if the rejection rate on real leaves stays high.
